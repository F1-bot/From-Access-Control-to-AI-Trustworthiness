## 3. The decision

For every unit, decide for each candidate target whether the target **requires** the outcome or measure that
the unit describes. Code from the point of view of an evaluation of a product (the evaluated system and its
developer), at catalogue level: a service counts if **any** of its levels requires the outcome, and an
assurance row counts if it is required at **any** guarantee level. You do not need to name the level, but you
may note it in `notes`.

- **F (full)**: the target explicitly requires the outcome or measure as a whole, so that an evaluation against
  this target would check it.
- **P (partial)**: the target directly contributes to the outcome, implements an essential part of it, or
  requires it only for part of its scope or in a weaker form, but it does not require the whole outcome.
- **none** (leave out): the target is unrelated, only thematically similar, or relevant only through
  organisational processes that the criteria do not require of the evaluated product.

Section 5 gives worked examples of all three grades.

Rules:

1. Credit a target only when the criteria themselves require (F) or directly contribute to (P) the outcome for
   the evaluated system or its developer. Organisational governance outcomes without a product-level
   counterpart (organisation-wide policies, workforce, culture, legal monitoring, external reporting by the organisation)
   are not covered by any target: leave the coding empty.
2. A unit may have several targets, including several F. Record every target that is F or P.
3. Use the most specific target that applies, but also record other targets that independently meet the rule.
   Do not add a target only because it is a precondition of another target (for example, NC-1 is a necessary
   condition of every service and is not credited for that reason).
4. The profile mechanisms are credited only for units whose outcome is a mechanism of the criteria themselves:
   `X:PROFILE` when the outcome is met by the legacy rating and profile scheme (choosing required service levels
   and a guarantee level); `X:AIPROFILE` when the outcome is the risk-proportionate selection of AI requirements
   or the statistical acceptance of quantitative claims (thresholds, confidence bounds at level 1-alpha_G).
   ATLAS mitigations are coded against services and assurance rows only.
5. Judge the unit text, not your expectation of how much the criteria should cover.

## 4. How to record

- `coding`: the targets with their grade, separated by spaces, written exactly as in the glossary, e.g.
  `NR:F IE:P G:OE-AI-1:F`. Assurance rows start with `G:`; the profile mechanisms are `X:PROFILE` and
  `X:AIPROFILE`. Leave `coding` empty if no target is F or P.
- `coder_flag`: write `NA` only if you think the unit is not applicable to the evaluation of a system (for
  example, an obligation of the Commission); leave `coding` empty in that case.
- `coded`: write `1` when you have finished the row, also when `coding` is empty. Rows without `1` are treated as
  not yet coded.
- `notes` (optional): the level you had in mind, doubts, or alternative codings.

Save the file as CSV (UTF-8) without changing the columns `item`, `id` and `framework`. Expected effort is two to
four minutes per unit.

## 5. Worked examples (units that are not in your sheet)

**Art.12(1)** (EU AI Act Arts. 9-15). *EU AI Act, Article 12(1). 1. High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system.*

- Coding: `NR:F`
- Reason: The legacy registration (audit) service NR requires the automatic recording of security-relevant events in a protected log, which is the whole outcome of the unit: full.

**MAP 2.2** (NIST AI RMF 1.0). *NIST AI RMF 1.0, MAP 2.2 (function MAP). Information about the AI system’s knowledge limits and how system output may be utilized and overseen by humans is documented. Documentation provides sufficient information to assist relevant AI actors when making decisions and taking subsequent actions.*

- Coding: `IE:F IO:P`
- Reason: IE-1 requires a model or system card that states capabilities and limitations, and instructions for use: full. IO-1 requires that oversight measures are documented in the instructions for use, which covers only the part of the unit on how outputs are overseen by humans: partial.

**GOVERN 2.1** (NIST AI RMF 1.0). *NIST AI RMF 1.0, GOVERN 2.1 (function GOVERN). Roles and responsibilities and lines of communication related to mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks are documented and are clear to individuals and teams throughout the organization.*

- Coding: `NO:P IO:P`
- Reason: Separation of duties (NO) and the assignment of oversight roles (IO-1) give the evaluated system defined roles, but neither requires the organisation-wide documentation of responsibilities for AI risk: partial for both.

**Art.12(2)** (EU AI Act Arts. 9-15). *EU AI Act, Article 12(2). 2. In order to ensure a level of traceability of the functioning of a high-risk AI system that is appropriate to the intended purpose of the system, logging capabilities shall enable the recording of events relevant for: (a) identifying situations that may result in the high-risk AI system presenting a risk within the meaning of Article 79(1) or in a substantial modification; (b) ...*

- Coding: `NR:P IE:P G:OE-AI-1:F`
- Reason: The AI assurance row OE-AI-1 requires a post-deployment monitoring plan with performance, drift and incident indicators, which is what the recorded events must serve: full. Registration NR provides the logging mechanism but does not require these AI-specific purposes, and IE-2 requires explanations to be recorded with decisions: partial for both.

**A.7.5** (ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A). *ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Annex A, control A.7.5 "Data provenance". Title only: read the control in ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Annex A (and its guidance in Annex B).*

- Coding: `ID:F`
- Reason: Documented data provenance is required by ID-1 (and attested provenance by ID-4): full.

- Coding: `(empty)`
- Reason: Understanding and documenting legal and regulatory requirements is an organisational governance outcome. No service, assurance row or profile mechanism requires it of the evaluated system or its developer: no target.

**PR.AA-01** (NIST CSF 2.0). *NIST CSF 2.0, PR.AA-01 (category PR.AA Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control). Identities and credentials for authorized users, services, and hardware are managed by the organization*

- Coding: `NI:F`
- Reason: Management of identities and credentials of users, services and hardware is required by the identification and authentication service NI: full.
